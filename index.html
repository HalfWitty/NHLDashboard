How it works

How the model works

Data. Every regular-season game this season and last (1499 games cached) is read play by play from the NHL's public feed: every shot with its location, every goal with its time, scorer and assists, lineups, faceoffs and penalties.

Shot quality (xG). A logistic model fitted on 126,585 shots turns each shot into a goal probability from distance, angle, shot type, rebounds and strength. Expected goals are steadier than actual goals over short stretches, so ratings blend 60% xG with 40% actual goals.

Current form first. Each game's weight halves every 10 games, so the last couple of weeks dominate. Last season's games get an extra 50% discount and fade out as this season builds. Every rating is also mixed with 5 games of league-average play so a hot or cold week doesn't get over-trusted.

Strength of schedule. Each game is judged against the opponent's rating (iterated), so scoring 4 on a strong defense counts for more than 4 on a weak one. Home ice uses the league's actual home/away scoring split (2.89 vs 2.77 non-empty-net regulation goals). Teams on the second night of a back-to-back get a small attack/defense penalty.

Simulation. Each game is played 20,000 times minute by minute. Scoring rates follow the league's minute-by-minute goal pattern, adjusted for each team's own early/late tendencies (pulled hard toward average). Trailing teams pull the goalie in the last 3 minutes, calibrated to the league's 0.37 empty-net goals per game. Tied games go to 3-on-3 overtime (65% decided in OT historically), then a 50/50 shootout. Totals and moneylines count OT and the shootout goal, like most books.

Players. Each skater's scoring rate blends goals and individual xG per game (half-life 20 games, last season discounted, shrunk toward the average forward or defenseman). A player's share of his team's expected goals, applied to the simulated team goal distribution, gives his anytime-goal chance. Points work the same way using his share of team goals he's involved in.

Fair odds and ranges. Fair odds are the no-vig price of the model probability. Bet at knocks the probability down by a cushion that's wider early in the season and for player props, so a price at or better than it is still a good bet if the model is somewhat off. Worse than fair, avoid.

Top bets. Sportsbook prices (moneyline, puck line, total) come from the NHL's odds feed and ESPN's public scoreboard. A high-probability bet (58%+) qualifies if the price beats fair odds; any other bet needs the model's cautious probability to beat the price. Neither can be a longshot (model 30%+), and the model isn't more than 12 points away from the market's no-vig probability, which usually signals news the model can't see. Qualifying bets alternate between high-probability picks and the largest edges. Markets without a fetched price go on the price-check list, ranked by how far they stand out from the rest of the slate.

Track record. Each run logs its pre-game probabilities to nhl_data/predictions_log.csv and grades them once games finish. Use the Track record tab to check calibration before trusting a market.

Limits. The feed doesn't announce starting goalies or injuries in advance; the model uses each team's recent results (which include its goalies) and the last game's lineup. Check confirmed starters before betting, especially on back-to-backs.
