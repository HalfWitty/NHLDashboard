How it works

How the model works

Data. Every regular-season game this season and last (1547 games cached) is read play by play from the NHL's public feed: every shot with its location, every goal with its time, scorer and assists, lineups, faceoffs and penalties.

Shot quality (xG). A logistic model fitted on 130,513 shots turns each shot into a goal probability from distance, angle, shot type, rebounds and strength. Expected goals are steadier than actual goals over short stretches, so ratings blend 60% xG with 40% actual goals.

Current form first. Each game's weight halves every 10 games, so the last couple of weeks dominate. Last season's games get an extra 50% discount and fade out as this season builds. Every rating is also mixed with 5 games of league-average play so a hot or cold week doesn't get over-trusted.

Strength of schedule. Each game is judged against the opponent's rating (iterated), so scoring 4 on a strong defense counts for more than 4 on a weak one. Home ice uses the league's actual home/away scoring split (2.90 vs 2.78 non-empty-net regulation goals). Teams on the second night of a back-to-back get a small attack/defense penalty.

Simulation. Each game is played 20,000 times minute by minute. Scoring rates follow the league's minute-by-minute goal pattern, adjusted for each team's own early/late tendencies (pulled hard toward average). Trailing teams pull the goalie in the last 3 minutes, calibrated to the league's 0.37 empty-net goals per game. Tied games go to 3-on-3 overtime (65% decided in OT historically), then a 50/50 shootout. Totals and moneylines count OT and the shootout goal, like most books.

Players. Each skater's scoring rate blends goals and individual xG per game (half-life 20 games, last season discounted, shrunk toward the average forward or defenseman). A player's share of his team's expected goals, applied to the simulated team goal distribution, gives his anytime-goal chance. Points work the same way using his share of team goals he's involved in.

Fair odds and ranges. Fair odds are the no-vig price of the model probability. Bet at knocks the probability down by a cushion that's wider early in the season and for player props, so a price at or better than it is still a good bet if the model is somewhat off. Worse than fair, avoid.

Top bets. Sportsbook prices (moneyline, puck line, total) come from the NHL's odds feed and ESPN's public scoreboard. A high-probability bet (58%+) qualifies if the price beats fair odds; any other bet needs the model's cautious probability to beat the price. Neither can be a longshot (model 30%+), and the model isn't more than 12 points away from the market's no-vig probability, which usually signals news the model can't see. Qualifying bets alternate between high-probability picks and the largest edges. Markets without a fetched price go on the price-check list, ranked by how far they stand out from the rest of the slate.

Track record. Each run logs its pre-game probabilities to nhl_data/predictions_log.csv and grades them once games finish. Use the Track record tab to check calibration before trusting a market.

Limits. The feed doesn't announce starting goalies or injuries in advance; the model uses each team's recent results (which include its goalies) and the last game's lineup. Check confirmed starters before betting, especially on back-to-backs.

Grades, trends and units

Grades. Every pick gets a score out of 100: edge at the price after the trust adjustment (30; price checks are scored at a price halfway between fair and Bet at), win chance (10), how close the model is to the market (10; a huge gap usually means news the model can't see), whether situational trends back the side (30) and how much the model's claimed edge has held up in that bet type (20). Totals and props have no side for trends to back, so they're scored out of the other 70 points. A is 66+, B 54+, C 42+, D below. Unpriced picks and early-season picks top out at B. Hover a grade for the breakdown.

Trend picks. Each side is checked for situational factors: a better record, scoring margin or recent form, a home/road split, the opponent on a back-to-back or coming off overtime, rest, streaks and the opponent's last loss being to a worse team. Each factor's record comes from past games, and only factors that won more than half the time (after mixing in 60 games of 50/50) count. A trend pick needs 3+ of them and a net edge over the opponent's factors. The "ways to bet it" ladder compares the moneyline, regulation or spread and team total against the model's fair price, and the play is the one with the best expected return.

Suggested units. 0.25 Kelly (1 unit = 1% of a bankroll), rounded to half units and capped at 3u. Before sizing, the model's win chance is pulled toward the market by the bet type's trust: trust = the share of the model's claimed edge that has actually shown up in graded results. It starts at 70% (the Bet-at cushion already covers model doubt) and moves only as far as the results can support: a few dozen bets barely move it, a few seasons can move it a lot. A bet type that has matched the market and not the model gets small or zero units.

Scatter. On the Track record tab every priced pick is placed by its edge at the price (across) and the price (up), coloured by result, with upcoming picks as rings. Zones shade areas where at least 5 graded picks made or lost money, so you can see whether tonight's picks sit where past ones paid.
